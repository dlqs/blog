Simulation Argument

October 30, 2026

The most convincing argument I’ve heard in favor of theism so far is the Simulation Argument.

But first, we need to talk about ancestor simulations. Simply put, an ancestor simulation is us, but in the future! To our faraway descendants, we will be their ancestors, and just like how we today are interested in how the cavemen lived, so too will our descendants will be interested in how we lived.

A key property of these ancestor simulations is that they are actually legitimate conscious experiences - the very same kind of consciousness you are experiencing right now - and not some watered down, game version. The real stuff. This means that we need to believe that consciousness can exist on things other than biological brains. Perhaps silicon chips?

(This is called subtrate independence, the idea that experiences like consciousness can exist independently of their physical forms. Numbers, for instance. But not everyone buys this!)

Okay, with that, the Simulation Argument goes something like this:

  1. Future civilizations can run many ancestor simulations.
  2. Future civilizations will run many ancestor simulations.
  3. Therefore, it is likely that we are currently in an ancestor simulation.

So: if our descendants become technologically advanced enough to run such simulations, and they in fact do so, many times, simulating entire universes over and over again, then the number of simulated consciousnesses would likely be several orders of magnitude higher than biological ones. And so we’re probably in one right now!

Okay, so there are several parts of this argument that can be contested.

(1) may not come true for several reasons: perhaps all civilizations may hit technological barrier eventually. Perhaps we will always hit a nuclear/AI world ending event that always sets us back to square one. Perhaps it’s just too difficult!

As for (2): perhaps future humans may find it evil to run ancestor simulations, which, after all, are supposedly so real! Or maybe they’re just not interested enough to find out how we lived.

Finally, if we are in an ancestor simulation, then our simulators are to us as we are to The Sims…

Either way, even though this doesn’t quite tip me past the 50% mark of p(theism is true), it does get me pretty close.
