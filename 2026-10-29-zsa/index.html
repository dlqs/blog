Learning two things at once

October 29, 2026

A while ago, on the advice of an older engineer who told me about the importance of preventing wrist pain, I bought a ZSA Voyager keyboard.

At the same time, I decided to learn Colemak-dh. Partly for ergonomic reasons (the layout is actually easier on the fingers), but mostly because I wanted to see if I could rewire my brain on a tool I’ve been using practically my whole adult life: my keyboard.

Obviously, this would make the switch to the split keyboard approximately twice as difficult, but I figured that I’d get over the pain for both in one go, and it’d be useful for my brain to have a “trigger” to switch between the layouts: split keyboard + Colemak-dh, laptop + QWERTY.

So for a while, my routine was: start my day with the split keyboard, try to send some messages with it, after 30 mins (if I get that far), become frustrated at typing at 30 wpm, then switch to my regular laptop keyboard. This lasted for two weeks. Then I put away the split keyboard in a drawer and forgot about it for the next six months.

Then one day I saw it and remembered, “oh yeah I spent $300 on that”, and pulled it out again. For whatever reason - maybe the Colemak neurons in my brain settled? - the second time was much better. When I hit roughly 60-70 wpm, I crossed the threshold where Colemak-dh could become my default layout, and finally updated my laptop keyboard to match, completing the cutover.

So yeah. Split keyboards are great! Just don’t pair the cutover with a layout change too, unless you’re like me, and like to torture yourself.
